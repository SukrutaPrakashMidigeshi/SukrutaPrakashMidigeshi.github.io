Many puzzles such as Tower of Hanoi are graph-reachability tasks whose graphs grow exponentially, yet their structure admits simple low-complexity algorithms. We show theoretically that faithfully executing such algorithms is easier for small transformers to express than general-purpose reachability algorithms, and empirically that giving these algorithmic hints to language and reasoning models substantially delays their accuracy collapse.
